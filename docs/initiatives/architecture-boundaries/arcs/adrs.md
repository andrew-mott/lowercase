## Change C5 - ADR(s) reflecting the settled, now-real shape - merged (PR #345)

### Discussion

- **Scope check before drafting: secrets/`SecretsPort` work and "tool" as vocabulary are both explicitly out of this Change.** Secrets was only fleshed out as a shape during Change C4's research, not built, and stays its own dedicated future push — not ADR material yet. "Tool" is likely not going to appear as settled vocabulary in these ADRs at all; what actually needs naming is whatever the worker's protocols/transports get called, and that's a smaller naming question than "is 'tool' the right word," not a blocker on writing the ADRs.
- **Before drafting, a real gap surfaced: the `Operations` tier has a sharp definition and a working diagnostic test, but no substantial real example.** Searched the codebase directly (not just re-reading the research doc) for single-port, real-work, synchronously-called candidates beyond the one already named in `INITIATIVE.md`'s "not yet scoped" section (`runFlow()`):
  - `addFlowToCas()` (`packages/use-cases/run-flow/src/add-flow.ts`) — genuinely one-port (`ArtifactsPort`), but ~10 lines, smaller than `runFlow()`, not a stronger example.
  - `packages/artifacts`'s `Artifacts` class — already flagged elsewhere in the research doc as needing to split (hashing/canonicalization → functional core). Checked what's left after that split: still real orchestration across **two** ports (`ArtifactStorePort` + `ArtifactRepositoryPort`, in `putBytesInternal`/`putIndex`) — the same "needs to decompose before it qualifies" shape as `create-fork-spec.ts`'s `startForkedSim()`, not a second confirming example.
  - The engine's effect handlers (`resolveBranchValueFx` etc.) — checked `EffectHandlerDeps` directly: it bundles `ef`, `runQuery`, `artifacts`, plus `enqueue`/`processAll`, a multi-port bag, not one port. This weakens (doesn't invalidate) the research doc's "confirmed twice" claim about Operation-dependency wiring — the _wiring mechanism_ (caller already holds the deps, threads them through, `runtime` never wires an Operation directly) is genuinely the same between `RunService`→`runFlow()` and `wireEffectHandlers`→effect functions, but effects don't satisfy the one-port test itself, so they were never a second example of the tier — consistent with Effects already being deliberately a different, separately-named concept, just confirmed structurally too now.
  - **Net finding: every real candidate beyond the one named tiny example is either equally tiny, or is actually more evidence of the _problem_ Operations exists to catch (multi-port orchestration wearing a smaller shape's clothes) rather than a confirmation of the tier itself.** The diagnostic test keeps correctly firing (`create-fork-spec.ts`, `Artifacts`); nothing has been built _as_ an Operation from the start.
- **Decision: write `Operations` into the ADR anyway, as a settled tier.** Reasoning, given directly: it's a good theory and a good guiding principle for refactors already anticipated (events, the worker itself, others) — some of that work may genuinely benefit from a single-port boundary even though nothing built that way exists yet. This is explicitly a best-current-guess, not a claim of proof — if it turns out not useful over time, the fix is superseding the ADR, not having avoided writing it down. Recorded as the reasoning specifically so it doesn't read, later, as if the tier were claimed proven when it wasn't.
- **Separate, more general worry raised: ADRs as immutable declarations, likely wrong at the time despite good effort.** Resolved by pointing back at this project's own already-established ADR practice ([[feedback_adr_practice]]): forward-looking only, never retroactively rewritten, superseded rather than edited when wrong. An ADR here isn't a permanent correctness claim — it's a timestamped record of the best decision made with what was known then, and supersession is the designed mechanism for being wrong later, not a failure of the practice. Directly unblocks writing `Operations` in now rather than waiting for a real example to exist first.

- **The previously-planned worker/tool extensibility guide is cut, not just deferred.** "Tools" dissolved as a concept per Change C4's own conclusion — there's no third-party-authored thing left to write a guide about. Separately, the worker's internal architecture is itself unsettled and a known future rebuild target, so writing a guide now risks documenting structure that's about to change underneath it. Decision: revisit only if tools resurface as a real concept during that future rebuild; until then this isn't scoped. The package-boundaries README (still planned, timing undecided) absorbs whatever's worth saying about the worker's protocols/transports as part of that component's own entry, rather than needing its own document. Standalone docs for significant components (engine, worker) are named as a plausible future idea, explicitly not now.
- **Resolved: the worker/tool-extensibility conclusion does warrant its own ADR** — it's a decision record about the _boundary_ (worker owns infra, no plugin system, extension = speaking a supported protocol), which needs to survive and constrain a future worker rebuild rather than be invalidated by one.
- **ADR count settled: two, not three.** `Operations` stays folded inside the taxonomy ADR rather than split out — the tension between "it's more speculative, splitting makes it easier to supersede independently" and "splitting breaks up the coherence of one discussed unit" resolves by not choosing: keep the taxonomy as one ADR (the coherent unit it actually is), but mark `Operations` within it with an explicit lower-confidence/status note distinct from the other seven tiers. Nothing requires whole-ADR supersession — if `Operations` needs revision later, a small follow-up ADR supersedes just that section, with a note added pointing to it; the other seven tiers stay standing. Worker/tool extensibility remains its own, second ADR (different research doc, different Change, different question — how the system is organized internally vs. how it's extended from outside).
- **"Is the worker/tool extensibility ADR actually earned?" — a sharper worry than it first sounds, tied directly to this initiative's own stated methodology.** `Evolution` in `INITIATIVE.md` explains the whole pivot to a moves/renames execution pass specifically because "discussion alone repeatedly misses things only real code surfaces" (borrowed from `ui-workspace`'s own `research/state-management.md` finding). Changes C1–C3 earned formalization that way: research, then real physical execution, seeing what broke. **Change C4 never got that pass** — research → arc → straight to "settled" → merged, no execution step.
  - Resolution, not a full pass but not nothing either: Change C4's central claims were checked against **real, existing code**, not just reasoned about — `httpjson.tool.ts` was actually read to confirm it doesn't forward job events (what retroactively killed envelope-shape registration), and "worker owns all infra, tool owns none" describes how `httpjson`/`mcp` already behave today. Same evidentiary bar Change C1's taxonomy used before its own moves/renames pass.
  - **The real, specific gap: the third-party-extension half of the claim has never been exercised.** "Someone else stands up a service, speaks a supported protocol, it just works, no registration needed" is a projection from confirmed internal behavior, not something tried against a real outside service.
  - **Decision: write the ADR, flag this claim precisely rather than delay the whole thing** — same honesty mechanism as `Operations`'s status note, applied to one specific claim instead of the whole conclusion. The internal half (worker owns infra, existing protocols behave this way) is confirmed-by-code; the external half (frictionless third-party extension) stays marked as unexercised/design-intent until something outside this repo actually integrates against it.

### What actually landed

Both ADRs were drafted, then line-edited together piece by piece against the actual text, not just discussed and handed off. [`docs/adr/0005-package-tier-taxonomy.md`](../../../adr/0005-package-tier-taxonomy.md): eight tiers, `Operations` carrying an explicit speculative-status note per the discussion above, plus a one-line "explicitly out of scope" pointer rather than a detailed list. [`docs/adr/0006-worker-tool-extensibility-model.md`](../../../adr/0006-worker-tool-extensibility-model.md): no tool registry, the third-party-extension half of the claim flagged as unexercised design-intent, distinct from the internally-confirmed half — needed almost no changes on review.

**Real corrections made during the line-edit pass, not just polish:**

- The opening Context paragraph originally framed the whole initiative around "which package does this go in" (an AI-assisted-work/placement-lookup justification). Corrected to what actually drove it: a deliberate audit to firm up tiers that were already unsatisfying (`Operations`, `functional core`, whether `services`/`components` were scoped and named right) — the placement benefit is a real secondary consequence, not the motivation.
- Two separate instances of "proven"/"deliberately proven against real code" language were softened. What actually happened was moving, renaming, and removing existing code and seeing whether the organization held — not building anything new to stress-test it. "Whether it holds is something only time will show" is more honest than "proof."
- The dependency-direction description in the Decision section's opening line was wrong twice over: first, listing `adapters` last implied it depends on `components`/`application services`/`Operations`, when adapters actually only depend on `types`/`ports`/`functional core` and are wired in solely by `runtime`; then, once rewritten to place `Operations` relative to `functional core`, it was phrased as "above" when the project's own convention (`CLAUDE.md`'s layering — more foundational = "above") meant it should be "below."
- `adapters`' "may not import an Operation" rule was stated with the same confidence as the confirmed "may not import another adapter" rule, when the research doc had only ever flagged it as an untested intuition — split into two separate confidence levels rather than one flat prohibition.
- The `packages/artifacts` example illustrating adapters' "infrastructure mechanics only" principle, and the "microservices" aside in `components`' naming rationale, were both cut as discussion detail that had leaked in rather than being load-bearing for the decision itself.
- The "explicitly not settled" section was rewritten from a multi-bullet, research-doc-level enumeration down to a one-line pointer at `INITIATIVE.md` — the sharpest correction of the pass: an _accepted_, immutable ADR shouldn't carry status detail (like `packages/replay`'s tier) that's actively going to change; that belongs in a living document, not a frozen one.
- All references to internal initiative Change numbering (`Change C2`, `Change C3`) were removed from ADR text — that numbering only means something inside this initiative's own docs, not to a reader encountering the ADR on its own later.
- Considered and declined adding a `## Summary` section (ADR-0004 has one, against the ADR-0001 template's own shape) — the package-boundaries README, still unscoped, is the better home for a fast human-readable version; duplicating that inside an immutable ADR risks the same drift problem the "not settled" trim above was fixing.

Both ADRs are considered ready to commit. `Operations` (ADR-0005) and the third-party-extension claim (ADR-0006) both carry explicit, on-the-record acknowledgment that they may not hold up over time — matching how this Change's whole discussion started: good decisions made on current best judgment, held resolutely unless something real pushes back, at which point the fix is appending/superseding, not having avoided writing them down in the first place.
